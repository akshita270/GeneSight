BRCA1 → Breast Cancer
BRCA1 normally repairs broken DNA. A mutation breaks this repair system — cells divide uncontrollably, causing tumors.
APOE4 → Alzheimer's
APOE4 affects how the brain clears protein waste. When it fails, toxic plaques build up — damaging memory and cognition.
LRRK2 → Parkinson's
LRRK2 helps brain cells manage waste. Mutations cause toxic buildup in neurons — destroying movement control over time.

Everything you need to know about GeneSight and how it works

Hypotheses are ranked by a confidence score (0–100%) based on how many peer-reviewed papers support each gene–disease link, the strength of co-occurrence, and pathway plausibility. "Strong" hypotheses (≥75%) have robust multi-paper evidence. All outputs should be treated as research leads to validate, not clinical conclusions.
GeneSight integrates four live scientific databases: PubMed (33M+ biomedical papers via NCBI E-utilities), NCBI Gene (gene-level annotations and synonyms), UniProt (protein functions and interactions), and Neo4j (graph database for storing and querying biological relationships discovered during the run).
Your query is sent to the backend pipeline to retrieve and analyse public PubMed literature — no private data is involved. The knowledge graph built during your session is stored temporarily in Neo4j for the duration of the run. No queries or results are logged or retained beyond the session.
You can enter a gene name (e.g. BRCA1, TP53), a disease (e.g. Alzheimer's disease, Type 2 diabetes), a biological pathway (e.g. mTOR signalling, neuroinflammation), or a combination. The more specific you are, the more targeted the hypotheses. Example: "LRRK2 gene role in Parkinson's neuroinflammation pathways".
Most queries complete in 30–90 seconds. The pipeline fetches up to 30 PubMed papers, extracts biomedical entities, builds a knowledge graph, generates hypotheses with GPT-4o, and validates them — all in sequence. You can watch each of the 8 agents complete in real time via the progress tracker.
